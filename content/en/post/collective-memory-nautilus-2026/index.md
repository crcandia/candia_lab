Why are some historical figures remembered across the world, while others remain familiar mainly within their own country? In *Nautilus*, Kristen French interviews César Hidalgo about four forces that shape collective memory: technology, territory, language and time.

The article discusses *Cultural proximity reveals a local premium in collective memory*, by Mariana Macedo, Melanie Oyarzun, Cristian Candia and César Hidalgo, published in *PNAS Nexus*. It also connects these findings with earlier research on how collective attention fades over time.
